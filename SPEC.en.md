## 1. Formal specifications of the upgrades

### 1.1 NbE with η (`nbe_eta.mbt`)

**Types and normal forms.** The simply typed language is `LTy ::= Bool | Unit | LTy × LTy | LTy → LTy`. Normal forms are **η-long βπ-normal forms**: `quote_eta` reads semantic values back by type:

| Type | η rule | Normal form |
|---|---|---|
| `A → B` | function η: `f ≡ λx. f x` | always λ-headed; apply to a fresh neutral and read back in `B` |
| `A × B` | product η: `p ≡ (fst p, snd p)` | always pair-headed; read the projections back in `A`, `B` |
| `Unit` | unit η: `u ≡ ()` | always `TmUnit` |
| `Bool` | no η (base type) | literal or neutral spine |

**Type-carrying neutral spines.** For η-expansion inside stuck terms (the normal form of `f:(B→B)→B` is `λg. f (λx. g x)`), application nodes of `TNeutral` store argument types and projection nodes store sibling types; `quote_tneutral` can therefore reconstruct a type at every spine position.

**Bidirectional procedures.** `ty_infer / ty_check` are syntactic bidirectional decisions; `eval_infer_t / eval_check_t` are their corresponding evaluations. Unannotated λs are not inferable; `TmAnn(t, τ)` supplies an annotation, erased during evaluation and absent from normal forms.

**Categorification.** Objects are `LTy`; morphisms are closed function terms modulo βπη; `nf_eta` is the canonical representative of every morphism. This is the **free cartesian closed category (free CCC)** generated by `Bool/Unit`. Composition `tm_comp_at`, identity `tm_id_at`, and associativity/identity/inverse laws are checked with `def_eq_eta` (`check_syntactic_category`). Untyped NbE (`nbe.mbt`) is the coarser βπ quotient of the same category: its normal form is the βπ-reduction of the η form. With product η, `swap ∘ swap = id`, previously true only pointwise, now holds definitionally at function type (`check_eta_swap_involution`).

**Decision and uniqueness.** `nf_eta(nf_eta t) = nf_eta t` (normal forms are idempotent); definitional equality is structural equality of normal forms. The simply typed fragment is strongly normalizing, hence terminating on well-typed input; ill-typed input is rejected by `ty_check` (`nf_eta` returns `None`).

### 1.2 General Glue comp (`glue_comp.mbt`)

**(a) Finite universe: the endpoint comp algorithm is general.** Input is an “equivalence line” `GlueLineFinite{a_line, t_line, e0, e1, face0, face1}`: arbitrary finite type lines `A0≃A1`, `T0≃T1` (presented by ua), endpoint equivalences `eᵢ:Tᵢ≃Aᵢ`, and arbitrary face formulas `φᵢ`. The prerequisite is the commuting naturality square `e₁∘coe_T = coe_A∘e₀` (`glue_line_coherent`; see the categorification). The algorithm is the endpoint form of textbook Glue comp:

```text
a_raw := coe_A(a₀)                                -- compose the unglued part in A
if φ₁ is unsatisfiable: (t₁, a₁) := (coe_T(t₀), a_raw)
if φ₁ is satisfiable:
    t₁ := coe_T(t₀)        (when φ₀ is satisfiable)  -- compose the partial part in T
        | e₁⁻¹(a_raw)      (when φ lights up only at 1)
    a₁ := e₁(t₁)                                   -- correction to e₁(t₁)
```

Correctness (`glue_comp_finite_correct`): the output is well formed in the target Glue type; if φ is satisfiable at both endpoints, coherence proves the correction constant (`a₁ = a_raw`)—the set-level form of “there is a correction path”; if φ lights up only at 1, the output base is still `a_raw` by construction. Incoherent data is rejected with `None`.

**(b) Eigensubspace lines: open-box comp and hcomp correction.** A “type line” `SubLine` is `j ↦ im(P(j))` for a Hermitian idempotent `P(j)`; an “equivalence line” `LinEquivLine` is `j ↦ U(j)` (rank-one partial isometry `a(j)t(j)†`, generally a pointwise linear-isomorphism matrix family). Given compatible open boxes transported into each other by `U` on a face (`glue_box_compatible`):

1. fill both boxes by ambient formulas, then prune pointwise by `P_T(j)`, `P_A(j)` (`fill_subline`, the retract Kan structure over a varying projector);
2. the raw cap need not satisfy `U(1)(t_cap) = a_cap`; form the correction square `(i,k) ↦ (1−k)·a_cap(i) + k·U(1)(t_cap(i))`, whose `k=1` edge is the correct Glue cap;
3. the difference vanishes on sides by compatibility, so prescribed endpoints do not move.

This square is exactly the `hcomp` performed inside `A` in the textbook algorithm, made explicit in the affine model. Soundness (`glue_comp_vec_sound`) checks boundary, membership in the subspace line, membership of the correction square in `A(1)`, and cap compatibility with `U(1)`.

**Categorification.** In (a), an equivalence line is a morphism of the **arrow category** `Ty²`: objects are equivalences `T≃A`, morphisms are commuting squares, and `glue_line_coherent` decides the square. In (b), `SubLine` is a path over objects of the Karoubi envelope (a continuous family of projectors) and pruning is Kan lifting for a retract whose image varies. Together: Glue comp = compose in A + compose in T + correct in A (hcomp), here separately visible as `coe_upath/coe_T`, the correction assignment, and the correction square.

### 1.3 Symbolic decision on the finite side (`bdd.mbt`, `wiring.mbt`, `system_symbolic.mbt`)

| Object | Old decision (enumeration) | New decision (symbolic) | Normal form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face sat/≤/≡ | 2ⁿ endpoint scan | ROBDD: `sat ⇔ bdd ≠ ⊥`; `f≤g ⇔ f∧¬g = ⊥`; `≡` is BDD structural equality | reduced ordered BDD |
| System quotient equality | pointwise lookup comparison | BDD effective regions `φᵢ∧¬(previous coverage)`, merged by value and sorted | `SystemNF` |
| System compatibility | enumerated intersection satisfiability | `face_sat` (internally BDD) | — |
| Finite equivalence wf/equality | elementwise 2^k check | wiring permutation check in O(#leaves); equality is wiring structural equality | signed permutation `Wiring` |
| `coe` round-trips | elementwise round-trips | wiring identity `w;w⁻¹ = id` | `Wiring` |
| Glue wf/soundness | pointwise/elementwise | `face_sat` plus one BDD witness; universal statements become section identities `w⁻¹;w=id` | BDD + `Wiring` |

Enumeration remains only in: (i) `elems_of` as the semantic function of a finite type (enumeration of the domain itself), and (ii) brute-force oracles in `*_extensional / *_oracle` functions and white-box tests used for **differential testing** of symbolic decisions; they are not part of any in-library decision path.

**Categorification.** BDDs are canonical representatives of face-formula free lattices; wirings are normal forms of morphisms of the **free symmetric monoidal category** generated by `id/not/swap/×/∘/⁻¹` (objects `Ty`, tensor `wiring_prod`, braiding `wiring_swap`, explicit associators/unitors; pentagon, triangle and hexagon laws checked by wiring equality, `check_wiring_coherence`).

**Complexity note.** ROBDDs can be exponential in the worst case (decision is nevertheless simplification-driven rather than a blind scan); wiring decision is linear in the number of leaves; `face_witness` reads a witness along one path of a BDD to ⊤.

### 1.4 Dependent NbE: Π, Σ, universes (`nbe_dep.mbt`)

The syntax `DTerm` carries both terms and types: `DUniv(i)`, `DBoolTy/DUnitTy`, `DPi/DSigma` (second components under binders), domain-annotated `DLam`, check-mode-only `DPair`, projections and `DAnn`; §1.7 adds dimensions, PathP, Kan operations, data and HITs, and §1.8 adds persistent environments, lazy values, semantic systems and generic schema constructors. In the semantic domain `DValue`, Π/Σ second components are closure functions `DValue -> DValue`, so dependent substitution is closure application; e.g. the type of `snd p` is `B(fst p)` by computation. Universes are cumulative, `Typeᵢ : Typeᵢ₊₁`, and Π/Σ are formed at the maximum component level.

Conversion and readback are by type, carrying both term and dimension levels: `conv_at/quote_at` perform η for Π/Σ/Unit/Path, and `conv_ty/quote_ty` handle type values as elements of universes. Neutral applications store argument types and neutral projections store sibling families, retaining complete type guidance in spines. The simply typed fragment embeds by `lty_to_dterm` (functions to Π, products to non-dependent Σ). Categorification: the syntactic model of categories with families, with context extension as parallel cons of value and type contexts, substitution as closure application, and normal forms as morphism/term representatives.

### 1.5 Kan structures for every project type (`kan_dep.mbt`)

The normal structure of a type line is `DLine{at, coe_fn, fill_fn}`, with `comp := fill(1)` and an outer `dcoe` wrapper making `coe(r,r)` return its input directly. Constructors recursively cover every type former: Bool/Unit/each Universe level are constant lines; Π coe is contravariant in the domain and takes the codomain over the transported argument path; Σ transports the first component and then the second along its path; fill/comp use the same recursion. A dependent family is therefore presented as `(path in A) -> DLine`, exactly the data required by dependent comp. Simple types (`LTy`) and finite types (`Ty`) embed through non-dependent families; finite ua paths give non-constant lines via `dline_of_upath`. The remaining project type families (eigenspaces and subspace lines, finite and vector Glue) take their Kan structures from existing modules and are jointly registered as `check_kan_all_types`.

### 1.6 Complete symbolic algebra for eigenvalues, projectors and affine filling (`algebra/symalg.mbt`, `algebra/spectral_symbolic.mbt`)

The exact scalar layer consists of rationals, multivariate polynomial normal forms, fractions and complex ordered pairs; matrix equality, Hermitianity and idempotence are polynomial identities. Eigenvalues are distinct formal atoms `λₖ`; spectral projectors are given by the Lagrange formula `Pₖ=qₖ(A)/qₖ(λₖ)`, where `qₖ(X)=∏_{j≠k}(X−λⱼ)`. Idempotence and orthogonality reduce to univariate identities `qₖ²−qₖ(λₖ)qₖ ≡ 0 (mod m)` and `qₖqₗ ≡ 0 (mod m)`, checked by monic division. Resolution of identity and spectral decomposition are the identities `Σqₖ/qₖ(λₖ)=1` and `Σλₖqₖ/qₖ(λₖ)=X`. Characteristic polynomials are exact expansions of `det(XI−A)` and are checked for a generic Hermitian 2×2 matrix, diagonal eigenvalue matrices and rank-one projectors.

Unique affine filling is proved directly with coordinate atoms `i,j`: four-corner bilinear interpolation and the expression produced by `square_fill` on affine edges are one polynomial; coefficients of the general ansatz `k00+i·k10+j·k01+ij·k11` are uniquely solved from the corners (difference formulas checked by polynomial equality), hence filling is unique in the bilinear class. For non-affine (polynomial) edges, the three boundary equations of `square_fill` remain identities in `i,j`, and projector pruning satisfies the exact identity `(I−P)(P·F)=0`.

### 1.7 The cubical AST: Paths, data, HITs and hcomp by type (`nbe_dep.mbt`, `kan_ast.mbt`, `ast_laws.mbt`)

**Syntax.** `DTerm` gains a fragment with separate de Bruijn spaces for term variables and dimension variables; `denv` is a dimension environment and `dlevel` is the dimension level. Type lines and tubes are bodies under one dimension binder, and systems are `Array[DTubeTerm]` with branches `d_tube(φ, body)`.

```text
PathP A a b       := DPathP(A, a, b)          Path A a b := DPath(A, a, b)
<i> e             := DLamDim(e)               e @ r      := DAppDim(e, r)
coe^j A r→s a     := DCoe(A, r, s, a)
comp^j A [φ↦u] a₀ := DComp(A, [d_tube(φ,u)], a₀)
hcomp A [φ↦u] a₀  := DHComp(A, [d_tube(φ,u)], a₀)
hfill A [φ↦u] a₀  := DHFill(A, [d_tube(φ,u)], a₀, r)   (value at dimension r)
```

Data and HITs live in the same AST: Bool (including `DBoolElim`), Nat, List and dependent eliminators; Circle `base/loop/elim`; Torus `base/loopA/loopB/square/elim`. Circle points normalize to `loop@r` (both `r=0,1` are base); Torus points normalize to `square@(i,j)` with coordinate `1` identified with `0` (opposite edges glued). The bidirectional checker requires every branch's `tube(0)` to equal the base on its face and overlapping branches to agree on intersections.

**Kan reduction.** `comp_value` drops bottom branches, takes a top branch's cap at `1`, otherwise samples the type line at a fresh generic dimension and dispatches by head former (`hcomp` is `comp` over a constant line):

| Type | Definitional reduction of `comp` |
|---|---|
| `Π x:A. B x` | **Contravariant**: result `λy. comp^j B_j(x_j) [φ↦u_j(x_j)] (f₀ x₀)`, where `x_j` is the target argument `y` filled backwards along the domain line |
| `Σ x:A. B x` | First compose the first component and take its filler `a_j=fill^j A [φ↦fst u] (fst a₀)`, then compose the second component over `B_j(a_j)` |
| `PathP A a b` | `<k> comp^j A_j(k) [φ↦u_j@k; k=0↦a_j; k=1↦b_j] (p₀@k)`: add moving left/right **endpoint branches**, making boundary laws definitional |
| `Universe Typeᵢ` | Empty tube is identity; nonempty `hcomp` returns semantic Glue `Glue[φ₁↦T₁; …; φₙ↦Tₙ] A₀`, retaining the complete DSystem; pack/unpack converts between `(partial, base)` pairs and endpoint values where faces decide, with multi-branch partials represented by `DPartial/DVPartial` |
| `Glue` | Compose in the base and each partial type branch separately, then **correct** by an hcomp in the target base type using partial fillers; when only a target branch lights up, its partial component is reconstructed backwards from the base along the corresponding Glue line |
| `Bool`, `Nat`, `List A` | Recurse by constructors: same Bool literal, Nat `zero/succ`, List `nil/cons` (head composed over the element line); `Unit` is always `()`. Open/inconsistent host systems remain formal `DNComp/DNHComp` |
| `Circle`, `Torus` | All-base systems reduce to base; nontrivial loop/square systems remain **formal hcomp points** (the standard stuck HIT composition) |
| Neutral type line | Do not force reduction: keep `DNComp(line, DSystem, base)`; quotation first merge-sorts the system by value and reads back all branches |

Filling and general coe are defined from comp: `fill^j A [φ↦u] a₀ @ r = comp^{j∧r} A [φ↦u; r=0↦a₀] a₀`, hence base at 0, comp at 1, and tube prefixes on existing faces; `hfill` is the constant-line version. `coe(r,r)` is identity, `coe(0→1)` is empty-tube comp, and reverse/arbitrary endpoints are reduced with `¬j`, `r∨j`, `s∨¬j` segments and fill (see `coe_value`). Top/bottom and intersections of semantic dimensions/faces are decided symbolically by ROBDD predicates through `dim_eq_sem`, `face_is_top_sem`, `face_is_bot_sem`, never by sampling.

**Elimination commutes with composition.** Dependent eliminators for Bool/Nat/List/Circle/Torus compute by the usual β-rules on constructors. If the scrutinee is a formal composition, take its filler, apply the eliminator branchwise, then compose over `motive(filler j)` (`elim_over_comp`). Thus even applying a constant-Bool-motive Circle eliminator to a formal hcomp point computes to the constant rather than sticking outside the eliminator.

**Categorification.** This is the syntactic core of a cubical CwF: contexts contain term and dimension variables; `PathP` is the dependent path object over the interval; each type former preserves Kan fibration structure by the lifting operations in the table. Data types are initial algebras with dependent elimination; Circle/Torus are higher inductive objects generated by points, paths (and the Torus commutation square), whose eliminators are sections of motives.

### 1.8 Semantic-system refactoring and generic schema generators (`nbe_dep.mbt`, `schema.mbt`)

#### 1.8.1 Persistent environments, lazy evaluation and semantic caches

**Environments.** Term environments `DEnv` and dimension environments `DDimEnv` are length-carrying persistent linked lists (`Nil | Cons(value, tail, length)`). `denv_cons/ddim_cons` allocate one node and share the old tail, so context extension is O(1) and old environments are never mutated; `DTVar(i)` and dimension variables look outward along the list. Declaration-order arrays are consed one by one with `denv_extend/ddim_extend` (the last declaration becomes index 0); public array boundaries (e.g. `deval(t, Array[DValue])`) convert once at entry through `denv_from_array`.

**Lazy values.** `DThunk{forced, compute, force_count}` is wrapped as `DValue::DVThunk`. `lazy_value` creates an uncomputed value; the first `force_value` runs `compute`, recursively forces the result and stores it in the same cell, so `force_count≤1` (observable via `lazy_force_count/lazy_is_forced`). The evaluator delays function arguments, pair and constructor components, eliminator cases, partial branches and generated constructor arguments, forcing them only when type-directed operations require them.

**Caches.** `memo_dim_line` keys dimension lines by the Debug representation after `dim_simplify`; `memo_family` keys Π/Σ families by the Debug representation of the argument quoted with `quote_at` at the domain. Tables are association lists inside closures; keys are stable through simplification/quotation. Generated schema telescopes and case types reuse the same evaluation, conversion and quotation infrastructure.

#### 1.8.2 `DSystem`, partial values and multi-branch Glue

Semantic composition branches are no longer passed as raw `Array[DTubeBranch]`, but packaged as:

```text
DSystem{ branches : Array[DTubeBranch] }
DTubeBranch{ face : Face, path : Dim -> DValue }
```

`dsystem_of/dsystem_singleton` remove bottom faces and simplify faces. Given a type line, `dsystem_canonical(level,dlevel,line,system)` further compares branch paths by type-directed conversion at endpoints and a generic dimension: convertible bodies merge with `FOr` face union, and remaining classes are sorted by quotation keys of generic bodies (then face keys). The AST checker applies this normal form to validated compatible tube systems, as do quotation and neutral comp conversion.

The value counterpart is `DPartialValue` / `DValue::DVPartial`, with AST form `DPartial(Array[DFaceTerm])`. `partial_normal_form` computes first-match BDD effective regions `φᵢ∧¬(previous coverage)`, merges equal values by `conv_at`, sorts by quotation, and returns `DSystemNF{classes, undefined}`; `tube_system_nf_at` samples a tube at one dimension. This parallels vector-system `SystemNF`, with arbitrary dependent semantic values as region labels.

The payload of `DVGlue` is `(DSystem, base, level)`. Universe `hcomp` places all type branches into Glue rather than dropping branches after the first; `DGlueParts.system` is the full system, `face` its join, and `line` only a single-branch representative line. Elements use `glue_partial_values` to align partial components to each type branch by semantic face and `glue_store_partial` to store them: raw for one branch, `DVPartial` for multiple branches. Checking, conversion, quotation and `comp_glue_value` handle compatibility, correction and lit branches branchwise. `DPartial` is an introduction form allowed only in checked positions such as the first component of a Glue pair.

#### 1.8.3 Generic inductive-family schemas

An inductive family is completely described by a finite descriptor `DInductiveSpec`: name, universe level, parameter telescope, index telescope and constructor list. Telescope and constructor argument types are `DTerm`s whose `DTVar`s point only to schema-local binders (parameters, then preceding telescope/argument entries). Every constructor argument is one of:

| `DArgKind` | Semantic argument type | Extra eliminator data |
|---|---|---|
| `DArgExternal` | a given external type | none |
| `DArgRecursive` | the same family at given index terms | the induction hypothesis for that argument |
| `DArgFunction` | `Π a:A. Family(index a)` | `Π a:A. motive(index a, f a)` |

Constructors also give result indices. `inductive_schema_validate` checks unique names, elaboratable parameter/index telescopes, and well-typed arguments and recursive/function/result indices; if an external type or function domain syntactically mentions the generated family, it is rejected, so only a conservative strictly positive fragment is accepted. AST forms are `DInductive(spec, params++indices)`, `DIntro(spec, ctor, params, args)` and `DInductiveElim(spec, motive, cases, params, indices, scrut)`; motives are nested λs abstracting indices and then the scrutinee.

The generator provides type formation, constructor inference, dependent elimination, telescope-stepwise type/value conversion and quotation. Elimination over a neutral scrutinee forms `DNInductiveElim` and quotes completely; over formal `DNComp/DNHComp` it samples parameters/indices along the type line and composes again in the motive. When the base and all tubes use one constructor, `comp_inductive_value` computes argumentwise fillers and substitutes earlier fillers into later argument types (the iterated Σ rule); functional recursive arguments are handled directly by the Π comp rule. Mixed constructors or non-intro scrutinees retain formal comp.

Built-in examples are `nat_schema`, `bool_schema`, parameterized `list_schema`, indexed `vec_schema` and `fin_schema`, plus `tree_schema` and `w_schema` with functional recursion. Laws cover Nat addition, List length, Tree leaf count, Vec/Fin index correctness, Π-shaped W induction hypotheses, and Nat/List/Vec comp.

#### 1.8.4 Generic HIT schemas

`DHitSpec` likewise contains parameter/index telescopes, but its generators are `DHitCellSpec{name, dim_count, args, result_index, faces}`, with `dim_count∈{0,1,2}` in the original generator and `faces` ordered `(dim0=0, dim0=1, dim1=0, dim1=1)`. `DHitFaceExpr` instantiates a face by a target cell, argument terms and dimension expressions; dimension variables are interpreted along `DDimEnv` (the last cell dimension is `DVar(0)`). Besides telescope/argument/positivity checks, `hit_schema_validate` checks every face's argument types, result-index convertibility and dimension-variable ranges; two-cells additionally validate all four corners under both reduction orders.

AST forms are `DHitType`, `DHitIntro(spec, cell, params, args, dims)` and `DHitElim`. The smart constructor `hit_cell_value` simplifies every dimension and recursively instantiates a face expression when one is an endpoint; conversion and quotation normalize endpoint cells first. Given a motive, `hit_case_type` generates arguments and induction hypotheses before producing a point type, PathP or nested PathP square type according to cell dimension, with endpoint values supplied recursively by lower-face elimination. Elimination applies arguments/IHs and then each actual cell dimension; neutral and formal comp scrutinees follow the inductive-family treatment. If base/tubes are always the same cell with fixed cell dimensions along comp, composition computes argumentwise; otherwise it remains a formal hcomp point.

Built-in examples are `circle_schema`, `segment_schema` (distinct endpoints) and `torus_schema` (base, two loops, commutation square). Laws cover endpoint normalization, Circle/Segment path elimination, Torus square elimination, neutral conversion/quotation, and the same-cell/moving-cell comp boundary.

### 1.9 Phase 6: closure of the original six boundaries

#### 1.9.1 Schema functors, mutual induction, quotients, universe schemes and higher HITs

Constructor arguments are no longer limited to three hard-coded shapes: `DConstructorArg` stores complete `DTerm` argument types and may carry dependent equality constraints of `DConstructorConstraint`. `DPositiveFunctor ::= Id | Const A | F×G | F+G | List F | Π(a:A),F` is a checkable positive-functor grammar; `positive_functor_type`, `positive_functor_map` and `positive_functor_ih_type` generate decoded types, recursive maps and decoded induction hypotheses. Nested recursion such as rose trees through `List` therefore follows the same introduction, elimination, conversion, quotation and comp paths.

Positivity decision is structured polarity recursion: traverse complete `DTerm`s, record occurrences of the family with positive/negative polarity and unknown neutral applications, expand transparent aliases during traversal, and reject negative occurrences or neutral family applications whose positivity cannot be proved. `DDecode` decodes Tarski universe codes back to types, letting a type family and its recursive decoding be generated simultaneously (an induction-recursion fragment). `DMutualFamily` compiles several components into one finite-Sum-indexed schema, recovering component types, constructors and eliminators by offsets and index equations. `DLevelExpr` and inductive/HIT level schemes represent universe levels as evaluable, revalidatable schemes. Quotients are HITs with equality 1-cells. HIT cells generalize to arbitrary finite dimension: on any endpoint coordinate, recursively substitute the face expression; elimination cases are dimensionwise nested `PathP`; comp over moving dimensions computes argumentwise when endpoints and target dimensions are exactly decidable, otherwise preserving formal neutral compositions as normal forms.

#### 1.9.2 Raw `DSystem` compatibility and first-match normal forms

Host-built `DSystem`s are no longer assumed compatible. `conv_at_under_face` turns faces into BDD-satisfying cubes and performs type-directed conversion in each fixed local dimension context. `dsystem_compatibility_report` checks that every branch agrees with the base at dimension 0 and that every pair agrees on its intersection, reporting the kind and branch indices of the first conflict. `dsystem_checked` and `dsystem_checked_of` are checked entrances into composition systems. For intrinsically first-match raw systems, `DTubeNF` decomposes effective regions as disjoint `φ₁, φ₂∧¬φ₁, …`, merges by value, and gives an exact semantic normal form; `dsystem_first_match_checked` promotes only systems that remain base-compatible after disjointification.

#### 1.9.3 First-class AST `Equiv`, `ua` and `GlueEquiv`

`Equiv A B` is an AST type former whose values consist of `fwd : A→B`, `bwd : B→A`, and section/retraction path proofs. Projections, `mk_equiv_term`, bidirectional checking, type-directed conversion and quotation recurse through all four components. `ua` sends a first-class equivalence to a path in a universe; its type line is the primitive `DVGlueEquiv`, and coe along it computes in the two directions as `fwd`/`bwd`. Every `GlueEquiv` branch stores a face, a partial type and a first-class equivalence to the base rather than only one type line; glue/unglue, endpoint collapse, PathP and Kan comp preserve this branch data.

#### 1.9.4 Checked type lines and the schema registry

`DCheckedTypeLine` stores a checked open type body, contexts, environments and a universe level. Its `at` is obtained by evaluating the body in a specified dimension environment, so it respects dimension substitution by construction; coe/fill/comp dispatch directly to the AST Kan engine. Raw host functions `Dim -> DValue` remain low-level semantic tools, but trusted entry points are delimited by checked lines. `DSchemaRegistry` interns inductive/HIT plans under stable keys, storing validation results, generic telescope types, and prefix chains with semantic argument snapshots; schema validation and telescope elaboration query tables first, and hit/miss counters make reuse observable.

#### 1.9.5 Algebraic extensions, generalized eigenspaces and Jordan theory

`QPoly`/`QMat` provide an exact layer of univariate rational polynomials and rational matrices (primitive pseudo-remainder gcd, extended Euclid, Yun squarefree decomposition, rational roots, characteristic and minimal polynomials, RREF). `QuadNum a+b√d` is a normal form for quadratic radicals (`d` squarefree, including denested square roots); `AlgNum` is represented by residues in `Q[x]/(m)` and can be rebased to an element's own minimal polynomial. Generalized eigenspaces are given by increasing kernels of `(A−λI)ᵏ`, algebraic/geometric multiplicities, indices and Jordan chains; primary projectors arise from Bézout resolutions of identity of coprime factors. When the characteristic polynomial splits over Q, the result is a full decomposition `PJP⁻¹=A`; non-split parts retain explicit residual factors; `AlgMat` moves the same theory to a chosen algebraic extension.

#### 1.9.6 BDD DAG scalability and certified numerics

The primary representation of face BDDs is a hash-consed DAG in `BddManager`: nodes are unique by `(rank,lo,hi)`, apply is memoized by `(op,a,b)`, equality inside one manager is root-ID equality, sat is root≠0, and size is reachable node count. `FaceDecision` makes exhaustion of a node budget a formal answer; variable order can be selected as the smallest DAG among index, first-occurrence, frequency and reverse candidates, and a step-budgeted DPLL serves as a sat/eq fallback. The exact numeric counterparts are `CertMat` over `QuadC = QuadNum + i·QuadNum` and coefficient-based `CertPath`/`CertSquare` of degree at most 2: projectivity, Hermitianity, orthogonal decomposition, eigenspace filling and corner coherence are checked by exact structural equality; the original `Double` projectors report only maximum deviation from exact shadows and floating residuals. `double_dyadic_interval` gives a rational dyadic interval containing a Double.

### 1.10 Phase 7: recursive declarations, S³, certified Jacobi, the HOU elaborator and modules

#### 1.10.1 Mutual induction, induction-recursion and nested recursion for arbitrary container functors

Mutual induction and Tarski induction-recursion use the compilation and `DDecode` of §1.9.1. Positive functors gain a dependent polynomial/container node `DFunctorContainer(Shape, Positions, index)` with semantics `Σ(s:Shape), Positions(s) → X(index s p)`; its IH type is `Π(p:Positions(s)), P(index s p, contents p)` and its map applies the eliminator positionwise. `container_tree_schema` itself is parameterized by Shape/Positions, so one generator carries arbitrary finite dependent-polynomial branching shapes and their nested recursion; shape/position types cannot mention the current family, recursive indices are checked under both shape and position binders, and mutual compilation tags indices with component labels.

#### 1.10.2 Sphere `S³`

`suspension_schema` generates north, south and a meridian path for every `x:X` from an index-free HIT `X`. `sphere2_schema = Susp(circle_schema)`, `sphere3_schema = Susp(sphere2_schema)`, and `sphere_schema(n)` iterates recursively. Endpoint meridians reduce at 0/1 to north/south; dependent elimination, conversion, quotation and Kan/hcomp all come from the generic HIT generator, and the identity eliminator computes back to itself on a generic meridian.

#### 1.10.3 Symbolic interval inclusion for Jacobi spectral iteration

An exact complex rational Hermitian matrix is first enclosed as a complex rectangular interval matrix. Each step selects a pivot from the midpoint matrix and represents the rotation's `|a|`, phase `a/|a|`, `c` and `s` by rational interval arithmetic and sqrt enclosures computed by integer bisection plus exact square comparison. Interval enclosures of a fixed unitary matrix are used for `J†AJ` and `VJ`, outwardly rounded dyadically after every multiplication to control denominators and symmetrized for Hermitian enclosures. Each certificate recomputes membership of coefficients, zero containment in `J†J-I`, inclusion of the similarity transform in the after-matrix, and inclusion of `VJ` in the transform-after matrix. Final eigenvalues are Gershgorin discs of the Hermitian interval matrix, grouped into clusters with multiplicities.

#### 1.10.4 Higher-order unification and the bidirectional semantic elaborator

Full higher-order unification is undecidable, so the implementation takes the Miller pattern fragment: a spine `?F x₁…xₙ` must consist of distinct bound variables, solutions abstract them, and the solver adds beta/eta/projection WHNF, rigid decomposition, occurs/scope checks, flex-headed applications and a closed-term fallback for non-pattern problems. A named surface language `STerm` inserts implicit metavariables through bidirectional `elab_infer/elab_check`; after solving, `hou_semantic_optimize` performs beta/projection/Nat simplification before translation to `DTerm`. The dependent motive of the Nat eliminator can be solved from an expected result type by HOU. Cores with unsolved metavariables are rejected, while successful results can be rechecked by the dependent checker.

#### 1.10.5 Parameterized modules, Open/Import and namespaces

A module is a telescope `params → imports → decls`. The elaborator recursively inlines imports: parameters are substituted by import arguments, target declarations become host telescope entries, and every declaration is checked with `d_check`; module cycles are rejected. Name bindings are separate from entries, so one entry can be resolved by a qualified name, an alias, or an opened/renamed name; `only/hiding` filter imports, ambiguous opens are rejected, and opaque exports become neutral across boundaries. `module_instantiate` is functor application: it simultaneously substitutes whole declaration segments by closed parameter arguments and checks each result in closed form, abstracting one proof segment for reuse at different types.

### 1.11 Phase 8: package architecture, cubical algorithms and computational univalence

#### 1.11.1 Packages and component dependencies (`architecture.json`, `algebra/`, `tools/arch_check.py`)

Files inside one MoonBit package have no compilation order; the project's ordering is a checkable dependency DAG. The physical package graph is `cmd/main → root → algebra`. Exact algebra is the independent package `vv0101v01/cubical-hermitian/algebra` and does not depend on root. Root imports the `@algebra` names it needs centrally through the non-public `using` declarations in `algebra_imports.mbt`, without re-exporting them as root API. `architecture.json` assigns every `.mbt` file to exactly one logical component (L0 `foundation` through L9 `app`); `tools/arch_check.py` validates unique ownership, physical package imports and approved component edges, and is the single tool source for file/line counts. `verify.mbt` is in the registry layer, so global checks such as `check_kan_all_types` can inspect both models and kernel without making the kernel depend upwards. [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) gives the rules and explains why `foundation` is not yet split physically.

#### 1.11.2 Cubical algorithms (`cubical_algorithms.mbt`)

`KanBox{line, system, base, dlevel}` packages a semantic type line, a `DSystem` and a base cap. `kan_box_fill` is its fill function (the base at 0, the composition at 1, and agreement with each tube on its face), `kan_box_comp` is the cap, and `kan_box_pathp` wraps the filler as a `PathP` value over the line. `transport_value` and `transport_back_value` are coe towards 1 and towards 0; `transport_checked_line_value` takes a line from a `DCheckedTypeLine`. `transport_filler_value` is `i ↦ fill(i)`, and `transport_pathp_value` wraps it as `PathP`: point `i` lies in `line(i)`, point 0 is the original value and point 1 is its transport. `check_cubical_algorithms` validates transport endpoints and checked lines, that an open-box filler is the base at 0 and agrees with tubes on faces, and that its cap equals the composition result.

Path values are dimension functions of `DValue`. `dpath_at_value` projects a point, `dpath_refl_value` is constant, `dpath_sym_value` reverses by `i↦p(¬i)`, and `dpath_prefix_value`/`dpath_segment_value` are the prefix `j↦p(r∧j)` and suffix/segment `j↦p(r∨j)`. For `p:x=y` and `q:y=z`, `dpath_trans_square_value(p,q)` constructs an explicit hcomp square whose base at composition coordinate `i` is `p(i)`, whose `i=0` face is constant at the start and whose `i=1` face is `q`; its top edge `i↦S(i,1)` is `dpath_trans_value`. `dpath_cong_value` is `ap`, `dpath_funext_value` swaps pointwise path data into a function path, `subst_value` is `coe^{j.B(p j)} v`, and `subst_pathp_value` is the dependent path whose far endpoint is that substitution. These algorithms dispatch only to the kernel `coe_value`/`fill_value`/`comp_value`; there is no parallel Kan engine.

At the closed-term layer there are builders `dtransport_term`, `dtransport_back_term`, path refl/sym/cong, hcomp-square `dpath_trans_term`, and `dfunext_term`; `check_cubical_algorithms` sends representative closed terms back through `dep_check_closed` and checks semantic endpoints by type-directed conversion. See the “Cubical algorithms” entry of `spec_registry`.

#### 1.11.3 Computational univalence (`univalence.mbt`)

**Finite universe.** `UPath` is itself presented by a wiring equivalence, so `upath_to_equiv` extracts that equivalence; `upath_refl/sym/trans` are its path algebra; and `finite_transport` transports along the path. `check_finite_univalence` checks both round-trips and transport in both directions by wiring structural equality on a repertoire of equivalences. In the `Bool`/`Unit`/product finite universe this decision is complete.

**Semantic AST.** For a semantic type line `L`, `path_to_equiv_value` uses `coe 0→1` as the forward map and `coe 1→0` as the backward map. The two round-trip proofs are composed by `transport_roundtrip_path_value` (and its reverse) from transport fillers, producing a first-class `DVEquiv`. `equiv_to_universe_path_value` is the named entry point, at this layer, to the Glue line of the existing `ua_value`; `ua_transport_value` is transport along `ua(e)`. `ua_beta_path_value` unglues the transport filler along the Glue line, yielding a path in `dst` from `equivFun e x` to `transport (ua e) x`; `ua_beta_back_path_value` obtains the reverse law from the partial component of the reverse filler. Nested Glue endpoint representations are normalized by recursive collapse.

The two round-trips are witnessed separately. `univalence_retr_path_value` interpolates the forward/backward functions by the `uaβ` homotopies; its section/retraction fields are assembled by path congruence, homotopy segments and the original proof, giving `pathToEquiv (ua e) = e` in `Equiv src dst`. `univalence_sect_square_value` is a two-dimensional Glue square: its `i=0` edge is the `ua(pathToEquiv L)` line, its `i=1` edge is `L`, and its `j=0`/`j=1` edges are constantly `src`/`dst`. Its interior is Glue over `dst`, with `j=0` branch `(src,pathToEquiv L)` and `i=1∨j=1` branch `(L(j), segment equivalence L(j)≃dst)`. `univalence_equiv_value` then packages `pathToEquiv`, `ua`, and both witnesses as a semantic `DVEquiv` certificate of type `(Equiv A B) ≃ Path (Type level) A B`.

**Checked lines and closed terms.** `UniversePath` stores a `DCheckedTypeLine`, fully collapsed endpoint types and a universe level; `universe_path_of_ua_term_closed` obtains a checked ua line from a closed equivalence term. `universe_path_path_value`, `universe_path_to_equiv_value` and `universe_path_normal_form = ua(pathToEquiv p)` give the path value, transport equivalence and normal form; `universe_path_transport_conv` decides univalence equality of universe paths when endpoints and transport equivalences are convertible. On closed terms, `path_to_equiv_term` builds a `DMkEquiv` whose round-trip proofs are the same fill/coe composites; `ua_map_term` and `path_to_equiv_map_term` are the two maps for the level-0 universe. `check_univalence` chains the finite side, semantic uaβ and both round-trips, checked universe paths, and closed-term acceptance.

**Statement scope.** `Equiv` remains a section/retraction quasi-inverse certificate. Kernel conversion for `DVEquiv` compares only fwd/bwd and treats proof components propositionally. The result is therefore an executable computational quasi-inverse univalence certificate checked by coe/comp/fill in this semantics; it is **not** claimed to be a HoTT formal theorem of univalence with the contractible-fiber characterization and all higher coherences, nor an axiom added to a logic.

### 1.13 Phase 10: general fiber contraction, internal π₃ presentations, bounded Huet search, module bytes and cost control

Phase 10 addresses the six items left after Phase 9, with the degree of completion stated separately for each. The general `lemIso` construction is a semantic-value construction; the sphere work consists of kernel types, canonical generators and a representative calculus, not a general homotopy-group classification for arbitrary elements; HOU is bounded search rather than a complete decision procedure for an undecidable problem; and the cost work enlarges the tractable fragment without removing worst-case exponential lower bounds.

#### 1.13.1 General `lemIso`, contractible fibers and automatic adjointification (`equivalence_phase10.mbt`, `kan_ast.mbt`)

For semantic quasi-inverse components `(f,g,ε,η)` and `y:B`, `equiv_fiber_type_value` is the homotopy fiber `Σ x:A, Path B (f x) y`. For arbitrary two semantic elements `(x₀,p₀)` and `(x₁,p₁)` of one fiber, `equiv_fiber_path_value` performs the standard three-layer `lemIso` square: hfills join `g(pᵢ(~i))`, the center `g y` and the retraction; a further filler joins their end edges; an hcomp rectifies the source square; and the image under `f` is closed by section-naturality squares to give the second component of the dependent Σ path. Discrete nested Bool compositions use `bool_cap_value` to expose the cap as a Bool value.

`equiv_fiber_contraction_value` specializes the path from the center `(g y, ε y)`, and `equiv_contractible_fibers_value` packages the resulting dependent semantic `(center, contraction)` function. `adjointify_equiv_value` retains `f`, `g` and the section; it replaces the retraction by the first component of contraction in the fiber over `f x` to `(x,refl)`, and extracts the half-adjoint triangle from the second square by a corner hcomp, returning `DAdjointEquivParts`.

“General” means that the formula is not specialized to a chosen equivalence or type: it consumes arbitrary semantic quasi-inverse components. It remains a host-semantic construction validated by conversion, not one closed `Π (e:Equiv A B), …` proof term submitted once to the dependent checker. Registered validation covers Bool negation at both points (fiber endpoints, bundled contraction, replacement retraction and both triangle endpoints), the primitive circle identity at target `(base,loop)`, and adjointification at the circle base. A generic midpoint of the circle contraction is checked only under the two endpoint faces of its second dimension, because no unrestricted open-midpoint reduction theorem for circle composition is claimed.

Acceptance: `check_general_adjointification_and_contractible_fibers`.

#### 1.13.2 Internal integers, π₃ quotient types and spherical loop representatives (`sphere_classification.mbt`)

`integer_schema` is a generated ordinary inductive type `IntegerS`, not a HIT. Its constructors are `zero`, `posSucc(n)` and `negSucc(n)`, where the schema-natural argument in the latter two is the absolute value minus one, so every integer has a unique constructor form. `integer_term`/`integer_value` give closed terms/semantic values, while `integer_succ_value` and `integer_pred_value` compute successor/predecessor on canonical constructor values semantically. Representative positive and negative values are typechecked and both `pred∘succ` and `succ∘pred` are conversion-checked.

`homotopy_group_type_term(A,a,n)` presents πₙ as the generated quotient HIT `QuotientS(Ωⁿ(A,a), λu v. ∥Path(Ωⁿ(A,a),u,v)∥₋₁)`. Thus `pi3_sphere_type_term(2)` and `pi3_sphere_type_term(3)` are internal types and both check against `DUniv(0)`; `pi3_sphere_point_term` maps a supplied closed 3-loop to a quotient point. Forming this quotient alone does not prove that every element is represented by the canonical loops below.

Canonical generators are recursive: `sphere_loop_power_value` starts with integer powers of the circle loop. At each suspension, `suspend_loop_value` applies the suspension unit pointwise and rebases with `subst_value` along the explicit `p ∙ p⁻¹` cancellation path built by `dpath_trans_sym_cancel_value`; it does not collapse a cube by replacing an endpoint pointwise. `SphereLoopExpr` is the free representative calculus generated by refl, generator, inverse and compose. `sphere_loop_expr_degree` is its integer-valued group homomorphism, `sphere_loop_expr_of_integer` sends an integer to the canonical generator-power expression, and `sphere_loop_expr_value` evaluates expressions in `Ω^dim(S^dim)`. The degree classifies this expression calculus (the decode/representative layer); values at `n∈{-1,0,1,2}` and one composite expression are checked only at their outer endpoints in S² and S³.

Consequently Phase 10 does not claim internal encode/decode equivalences `π₃(S³)≅ℤ` or `π₃(S²)≅ℤ` for arbitrary elements, and it does not turn the classical Hopf map/linking number into an internal Hopf-invariant proof.

Acceptance: `check_sphere_loop_representatives_internal`.

#### 1.13.3 Bounded Huet search (`hou_elaborator.mbt`)

`hou_search_unify` layers transactional search over deterministic `hou_unify`. Every node first tries the deterministic fragment; failure restores a clone of the `Unifier`, and any non-solved outermost result restores the input unifier exactly. Branches comprise imitation templates for closed rigid heads and term formers (unknown subterms become fresh metas over the formal spine and template binders); projections at every formal position, applied to zero, one or two fresh arguments; one eta-generation step; a constant guess; rigid decomposition, including eta expansion of a non-λ against a λ; attempts on either side of distinct flex-flex pairs and a common constant. For same-head flex-flex problems, positions whose two arguments can soundly be made equal are retained as invariant coordinates. A permutation with no invariant coordinate therefore receives a common constant. This pruning supplies one sound specific unifier, not an MGU and not an assertion that the unknown function intrinsically depends only on the retained positions.

The public defaults are `max_nodes=1000` and `max_depth=4`; at this entry point `max_nodes≤0` immediately reports budget exhaustion. `HouSearchReport` records solved metas, nodes, branches and maximum search depth. Its statuses are `HouSolved`; `HouNoUnifier`, reached when deterministic unification failed and no unsolved meta remains (for example a closed Bool-literal clash); `HouResidual` for an unresolved problem after its branch/depth allowance; and `HouBudgetExceeded`. Checks include imitation plus projections over a non-variable rigid spine, full and partly invariant same-head permutations, exact transactional restoration under a tiny budget, and a closed clash. Full HOU is undecidable, so neither completeness nor most-generality is claimed.

Acceptance: `check_hou_bounded_search`.

#### 1.13.4 Stable binary format for compiled modules (`module_binary.mbt`)

Version 1 begins with a 20-byte little-endian header: bytes 0..8 are `CHMODBIN`; 8..10 is the u16 format version (currently 1); 10..12 is a u16 flags field (currently required to be 0); 12..16 is the payload length as an i32 bit pattern; and 16..20 is the DJB2-xor payload checksum. Exactly the payload follows. The API represents bytes as `Array[Int32]` with each element in `0..255`, suitable to write unchanged; hexadecimal text is an alternative representation of the same bytes. The codec itself provides no filesystem API.

The payload stores every `CompiledModule` field in order. Integers are fixed-width little-endian i32, Booleans are one byte 0/1, and strings are an i32 count of UTF-16 code units followed by little-endian units. Every current `DTerm` constructor and the referenced `Dim`, `Face`, level-expression, positive-functor, inductive and HIT descriptors have fixed one-byte recursive tags; adding a tag requires a new format version. Parsing bounds each count by the remaining payload, rejects recursion depth over 1024, and requires exact payload exhaustion. `ModuleBinaryStatus` distinguishes bad magic, unsupported version/flags, truncation, checksum mismatch, malformed data, trailing bytes and semantic validation failure. `compiled_module_from_binary` is structural only, whereas `compiled_module_load_binary` also runs `compiled_module_validate`, rechecking hashes and re-elaborating the synthetic definition. #### 1.13.5 Extreme-cost controls: BDDs, watched-literal DPLL and fast QPoly multiplication (`bdd_dag.mbt`, `face_sat_watched.mbt`, `algebra/qpoly.mbt`)

Node and apply-cache tables start with 1024 buckets. The node table doubles once node count exceeds four times its bucket count, and the apply table doubles once `apply_misses` exceeds four times its bucket count; `bdd_manager_cache_stats` exposes both bucket counts and the apply-entry count. `bdd_dag_of_face_optimized` starts from the best existing candidate order and performs rebuild-budgeted sifting: each variable is moved to every insertion position, reachable-node counts are compared, and only strict improvement is adopted. `BddOptimizedBuild` reports initial/final reachable counts, attempts and whether any improvement occurred. It is a local heuristic, not a globally optimal variable order or a minimum-BDD theorem.

`face_sat_watched_dpll` bounds a `Face` with `face_bound`, compiles the face and dimensions inside its atoms to a Boolean expression, then to CNF with Tseitin extension variables. Search uses two-watched literals, saved phases, conflict-activity variable choice and ordinary DPLL backtracking (no clause learning). Positive witnesses are rechecked by `face_holds` before release. At this entry point `max_steps≤0` means no step bound, while an exhausted positive budget returns the explicit budget status. Equality is decided on the symmetric difference, and both top-level `face_sat_decide` and `face_eq_decide` now fall back to this watched solver. Seventy-nine generated seven-variable face pairs are differentially checked against `2^7` brute force, PHP(4,3) is proved unsatisfiable by the search, and PHP(3,3) returns a rechecked witness. Worst-case search remains exponential.

`qpoly_mul` selects a path from nonzero coefficient work. If the nonzero-pair count is at most 4096 or at most one quarter of the dense length product, it uses zero-skipping rational convolution. Otherwise it scales each polynomial once by the least common multiple of its denominators to a BigInt integer polynomial, uses the schoolbook kernel when the shorter input has fewer than 24 coefficients or the length product is at most 4096, uses recursive Karatsuba otherwise, and divides by the product of the two denominators. `qpoly_pow` uses binary exponentiation. The public check compares two deterministic 97-coefficient products coefficientwise against an independent schoolbook integer convolution and verifies `(x+1)^37`. Coefficient bit growth, hard factorization, close-root subdivision and splitting towers retain their intrinsic costs.

Acceptance: `check_bdd_cost_optimizations`, `check_qpoly_fast_arithmetic`.

`DTermInternTable` now stores child ids per node, so a reachable subgraph exports directly as `DTermDagSnapshot`: topologically ordered `DTermDagNode{shell, children}` records (a local shell is the node shape with its direct subterms abstracted) plus `roots`. Snapshots validate before import — child ids in range and strictly smaller, shell arity matching the actual child count, roots in range. Import hash-conses node by node against `(shell, destination child ids)` and reports `DTermDagImportReport{added_nodes,reused_nodes,node_ids,root_ids}`; re-importing the same graph adds zero nodes and returns the same roots.

The stable byte form is `CHDAGBIN` v1: 8-byte magic, u16 version, reserved u16 zero, i32 payload length and a DJB2-xor checksum; the payload writes each shell in the bare back-reference-free term codec followed by its child-id edges and the root ids. Sharing is thus the DAG's own edges, not a second byte-level scheme. Loading gives precise bad-magic/version/truncation/trailing/checksum/malformed statuses. In the law, three roots have fewer DAG nodes than tree nodes, round-trip field-equal, can be stored from one live table through the artifact repository and imported into another, and are gone after removal. Scope is explicit: `CHMODBIN` v3/`CHREGBIN` v2 still use payload-local tag-255 back-references; only `CHDAGBIN` shares the live interner graph. Acceptance: `check_interned_dag_byte_sharing`.

#### 1.16.5 Exact algebra: joint degree-pattern certificates and a four-generator multiquadratic tower (`algebra/qpoly_certificate.mbt`, `algebra/multiquad_tower.mbt`, `algebra/biquad_denest.mbt`)

Irreducibility certificates gain a portable `CertModDegreePatterns(primes)`. The checker requires a duplicate-free list of primes for which `mod_distinct_degrees` is usable (returns `Some`), recomputes each mod-`p` irreducible-factor degree pattern, and tests possible proper factor degrees (by complement symmetry it is enough to test `d≤n/2`): such a factor would force `d` to be a sub-sum of every local pattern, so if each `d` is excluded by at least one prime, the polynomial is irreducible over Q. For `x⁴−4x³−3x²−x−4`, the patterns are `[1,3]` mod 2 and `[2,2]` mod 3 — neither alone excludes both degrees 1 and 2, jointly they do; it has no single-prime or shifted-Eisenstein certificate in the search bounds yet factors with zero Kronecker budget through the new fast path. Duplicate primes, the composite 9 and either prime alone are rejected.

The quadratic radical tower rises to `MultiQuadNum`: rational coefficients on the product basis `√∏_{i∈S} d_i` for at most four generators independent in `Q*/Q*²` (16 slots). Binary operations align bases in the squareclass span and refuse a compositum needing a fifth generator; inverses come from Galois-conjugate products and signs from dyadic exact interval refinement. In-field square roots use relative-norm recursion (`x=u+v√d` forces `u²−dv²` square in the lower field, then `p²=(u±s)/2`, `q=v/(2p)`, all candidates squared-checked). One-generator extensions first find a rational square adjustment `x=γw²`; because γ is unique only modulo rational square classes already square in the field, the recursion normalizes it with `multiquad_reduce_gamma` before adjoining `√γ`, still within four generators. The square-root fragment of `radical_equal_exact` uses this tower. Radicands with no handled in-field/rational-square-class form, genuinely non-multiquadratic quadratic extensions, general algebraic radicands and fifth generators remain explicit non-goals. Acceptance: `check_qpoly_degree_pattern_certificates`, `check_multiquadratic_tower`.

#### 1.16.6 Search and numerics: exact orders to sixteen variables, DRUP, Tier D and adaptive Jacobi (`bdd_dag.mbt`, `face_sat_watched.mbt`, `dcube_filler.mbt`, `algebra/root_isolation.mbt`)

The exact BDD order DP no longer generates parent cofactor sets assignment by assignment. Child cofactor sets are built incrementally through the hash-consed `bdd_dag_restrict`, raising the provable cap from 12 to 16 variables. A fourteen-variable pair function is exact with `tried=114688=14·2¹³`, is a full permutation, agrees on every assignment and is no larger than the heuristic build; 16 is in-cap and 17 honestly reports `exact=false`.

CDCL proof output becomes DRUP with real deletion steps. Results carry chronological `FaceCdclProofStep = CdclProofAdd | CdclProofDelete`, with clauses identified up to sorted literal-set identity. The solver records learned clauses and actual database deletions in order (including the final empty clause); `face_cdcl_drup_check` independently maintains the evolving active database, RUP-checks each addition by unit propagation, and requires every deletion to name a currently active clause. It rejects deleting an absent clause, deleting before adding, and deriving falsity from an already deleted clause. On PHP(6,5) and PHP(7,6), Add/Delete counts reconcile exactly with `learned+1`/`deleted`, with positive deletion on the larger instance.

Stratified host-cube extensionality adds axis-specific deep-connection Tier D probes beyond face interiors, diagonals and ordinary connections. Cubes differing only on a `¬(s∧t)` shape, or only on the second axis at `(s∨t)∧¬s`, pass the old expression shapes but are separated; an equal two-dimensional pair runs all 105 probes, while arity five skips the oversized Tier D and reports 2291 probes with `complete=false`. This remains finite probing, not an extensionality proof.

Jacobi gains an adaptive driver. It first runs cluster-aware interval iteration; that path meets the target only when every Gershgorin cluster is a singleton no wider than the target. Otherwise it automatically falls back to exact characteristic-polynomial/Sturm cluster separation (doubling exact bits as needed) and records fallback use, exact bits, maximum width and `target_met` in `JacobiAdaptiveReport`. An easy example stays on the Jacobi path; eigenvalues `±1/4096` with a `1/16384` target and deliberately capped Jacobi precision stagnate and are then separated exactly. If the exact-bit cap is insufficient, the best separation is returned with `target_met=false`. Acceptance: `check_bdd_optimal_order_dp_extended`, `check_cdcl_drup_proof_with_deletions`, `check_dcube_stratified_tier_d`, `check_jacobi_adaptive_exact_fallback`.

#### 1.16.7 Sphere representatives: inverse/associativity 3-cells and classification of closed expressions (`cubical_algorithms.mbt`, `sphere_classification.mbt`)

Beyond the Phase-12 unit laws, loop composition now has explicit left/right inverse 3-cells (`α⋅α⁻¹`, `α⁻¹⋅α → refl`, by connection cancellation) and an associativity 3-cell. The latter uses `dpath_triple_square_value` to parameterize triple transitivity so the left- and right-associated transitivity squares share bottom and side edges, then fills between them with `hcomp` to expose the path of top edges; `sym(α⋅β) → sym(β)⋅sym(α)` is assembled from unit, inverse and associativity cells by the usual uniqueness-of-inverses conjugation. Faces and midpoint endpoints are conversion-checked on S² degrees ±1 and S³, including mixed-sign associativity examples.

This turns degree classification of the closed representative language into a witnessed classification. `SphereLoopExpr` (refl/generator/inverse/compose) has degree as its invariant and `sphere_loop_canonical_value` as normal form; `sphere_loop_expr_normalize3_value` recursively assembles a 3-cell from any such closed expression to the canonical word of the same degree — generator through the unit cell, inverse through symmetry-of-composite, composition through horizontal composition and canonical power multiplication. Thus `g⁻¹⋅(g⋅g)` normalizes and is joined to `g` on S² and S³, while a different degree is not in the same class. What is classified is precisely this closed expression language: reduction of arbitrary neutral loops to representatives, general `π_n(S^m)`, Eckmann–Hilton/commutativity and higher coherence remain open. Acceptance: `check_loop_inverse_assoc_and_classification`.

#### 1.16.8 HOU residual solving and the artifact integrity chain (`hou_elaborator.mbt`, `artifact_repository.mbt`)

Phase 12 could return residual HOU constraints, but callers had to resume them manually. `hou_solve_constraints` now solves a constraint *set*: repeatedly propagate the principal fragment through all equations, and only when stuck choose one residual for transactional iterative-deepening Huet search, feeding any specific guess back into global propagation. Before reporting `HouSolved`, every original constraint is rechecked under the substitution. A guess that makes another equation fail without proving the whole set unsolvable yields residual, not a false no-unifier; a Bool clash after a principal assignment yields `HouNoUnifier` with the input restored; exhausted node budgets yield `HouBudgetExceeded` with the residual. `HouResidualSolveReport` records initial/solved counts, search rounds and search counters. The law feeds exactly the `.residual` returned by `hou_unify_mgu` for a rigid-spine equation back to the solver and verifies reconstruction, solves a rigid-plus-repeated-spine pair, takes zero search rounds on a principal example, and leaves the blocked meta unassigned on a one-node budget. Search successes are specific unifiers, not general MGUs, and undecidability stands.

Artifact manifests now form an append-only integrity chain. Every successful put/remove first validates the existing chain and then appends a `CHARTCHN` entry (sequence, tombstone flag, the complete record, previous digest and this entry's dual-hash digest) to `chain.bin`; a corrupt chain makes writes fail closed. `artifact_repository_chain_report` reverifies every link, replays puts-overwrite/removes-tombstone to live records, and checks coverage against the index. Reads and recovery accept `expected_head~ : (Int32, Int32)?` as an external trust anchor — an Option, not a `-1` sentinel, because overflowed Int32 hashes can be negative. The law covers one blob under two names plus overwrite (entries=3/live=2), rejection of the old head after removal (entries=4/live=1), recovery of exactly one record after destroying the index without resurrecting the tombstone, rejection of an old-chain replay (recovery −1), invalidation by either record-hash or blob tampering, and bootstrapping a chainless legacy index's two records on its next put (entries=3/live=3). The dual checksum is not a cryptographic signature: the chain proves consistency relative to an externally kept head. Acceptance: `check_hou_residual_solver`, `check_artifact_manifest_integrity_chain`.

## 4. Boundaries remaining after Phase 13

- **Schemas.** SelfFamily parameter strict positivity is decided inside the `DPositiveFunctor` grammar by a mutual least fixed point, but opaque-operator positivity declarations are still trusted inputs; the lambda-verified variant covers only structurally visible functors. Functor mapping covers foreign IHs at nested arguments in the positive-functor fragment; decoded positions, shapes outside that grammar and genuinely ungatherable parameter occurrences are still refused. The simultaneous-IR eliminator route remains predicative (function level strictly below base level), and its union with the decode route is incomplete: forward references, non-universe foreign functions, level mismatches and cyclic mutually dependent blocks have no general compilation.
- **Raw `DSystem`, conversion and composition.** Relevant-dimension descent removes irrelevant dimensions and satisfying paths, but cost can still be exponential in the genuinely relevant support. Maximum compatible completion is maximum clique: on node-budget exhaustion it guarantees only an incumbent no worse than the sequential result, reports `exact=false`, and does not enumerate other maxima. Difference-guided Bool splitting remains a term-level tool rather than kernel `conv_at`, is exponential in the number of differing Bools, and has no Nat analogue. Square-valued Circle/Torus composition computes only when one candidate coordinate verifies against every branch face; arbitrary host data, and boxes agreeing only pointwise at endpoints, can still stop at `DNComp`, so composition for arbitrary-dimensional data on the primitive HITs is not claimed complete.
- **Persistence.** `CHDAGBIN` now makes bytes and the in-process interner share one DAG, but `CHMODBIN` v3/`CHREGBIN` v2 remain payload-local back-reference formats; there is no graph shared across all three formats and no incremental delta. Storage is still an abstract key→bytes protocol, with no OS filesystem backend, fsync durability policy, network transport or multi-writer concurrency. The `CHARTCHN` dual hash is a checksum, not a cryptographic signature: it proves consistency of chain, index and blobs relative to an externally kept head, and cannot establish authenticity if an attacker can replace both the store and that trust anchor. Legacy bootstrapping trusts the index at migration time; pre-chain history is not auditable.
- **Exact algebra.** Joint degree patterns are one certificate family among several; with no certificate, worst-case cost is still Kronecker enumeration and BigInt bit length, and certificates decide irreducibility without accelerating general factorization output. `MultiQuadNum` stops at four squareclass-independent generators. A fifth generator, a radicand with no handled in-field or rational-square-adjustment form, a genuinely non-multiquadratic quadratic extension, or a general algebraic radicand still returns `None`; the pure-cubic route remains separate and `radical_to_multiquad` does not absorb non-square roots.
- **BDDs, SAT, host cubes and Jacobi.** The exact BDD order DP now has a hard cap of n≤16, but remains `O(n·2^n)`-scale in work and memory. CDCL proofs are DRUP, not DRAT: they audit the chronological Add/Delete steps for genuinely learned clauses, do not admit arbitrary RAT introductions, and full replay has its own cost. Tier-D host-cube checking is still finite probing; higher arities skip the tier with `complete=false`, and unprobed dimension expressions or host state can in principle hide divergence. The adaptive Jacobi fallback pays characteristic-polynomial/Sturm costs, reports the target unmet if its exact-bit cap is insufficient, and does not remove the fundamental costs of high multiplicity, tiny separations or interval inflation.
- **Spheres and loop classification.** Unit, inverse, associativity and symmetry-of-composite now have representative 3-cells, and the closed `SphereLoopExpr` language normalizes to canonical words of the same degree. The classification lemma reducing an arbitrary neutral loop to a representative is still missing, so a complete internal `π₁(S¹)≃ℤ`, general `π_n(S^m)`, Eckmann–Hilton/commutativity and higher coherences such as the pentagon are not constructed. An internal Hopf map, Hopf invariant and `π₃(S²)` classification remain beyond representative evidence.
- **HOU and modules.** The residual solver interleaves principal propagation with bounded Huet search and reverifies the whole equation set on success, but search generally returns a specific unifier, not a complete set of unifiers or an MGU for non-pattern shapes; residual/budget outcomes do not imply unsatisfiability, and general HOU remains undecidable. Version negotiation and artifact transfer remain protocol models: undeclared future formats are unsupported, and there is no identity signing or remote transport.
